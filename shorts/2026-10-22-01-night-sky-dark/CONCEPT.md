# Why Is the Night Sky Dark? (Olbers' paradox) — visual concept (PLAYBOOK 5.0a/5.0b, written BEFORE building)

## What an ordinary viewer must SEE, even with the sound off
1. In a thick forest, wherever you look there is a tree: a small "gaze ring" at the centre of the frame always sits on a trunk as the camera pans. No gaps.
2. The forest turns into space: the trunks become stars, layer by layer (near stars big, far stars small), and the same gaze ring now sits on a star every time. More and more layers of stars appear until there is no gap left: the whole sky is one blazing sheet of star-surface light.
3. Hard cut to the REAL night: black sky, a few stars, wide dark gaps. The gaze ring now rests on empty black. (That is the puzzle.)
4. "It isn't dust": a dust cloud drifts in front of the stars, soaks up their light, heats up and starts to glow itself, so the sky would still be bright.
5. Poe (credited 1849 daguerreotype, public domain), name + 1848.
6. The answer as a picture: a map of space seen from above. We are the dot in the middle. Every star has a thin line of light travelling towards us. A ring around us marks how far light has had time to travel. Stars inside the ring are lit (their light has arrived); stars outside are hollow ghosts (light still on its way). The ring grows to "13.8 billion years" but most stars are still outside it.
7. Back on the ground: the dark gaps between the stars are filled with hollow ghost stars whose light is still travelling.
8. A person on a hill looks up; thin lines of light run from a few stars down to them: they are seeing the past.

## Look
Real-feeling night world: warm near-black (no navy, no neon), a misty dark forest, a real-looking night sky, cream starlight, ember-orange only for the glowing dust. Language of the Rubin Short kept where it helps: a hollow ghost = something that should be there / has not arrived. No graphs, no tables, no charts. Dark end card (cream on near-black) matching the video.

## Staging variety (differs from Loftus/AI/Rubin)
Forest -> morph into stars (one continuous parallax pan with a gaze ring), hard cut to a quiet real sky, dust moment, portrait beat, top-down light-travel map, back to the ground, final person-under-the-sky shot.

## Accuracy guards
- Say "mostly" (finite age is the main reason; Harrison). Poe "guessed", not "first". Expansion is not claimed as the answer.
- 13.8 billion years = age of the universe; the ring is light-travel distance, illustrative (not to scale, no expansion drawn).
- The dust shot: Herschel (1848): an absorbing medium would heat up and radiate (Harrison review).
- Blazing sky = overlapping stars covering every direction (Cheseaux/Olbers), stylised: real stars are tiny; shells of smaller, more numerous stars fill the sky.
