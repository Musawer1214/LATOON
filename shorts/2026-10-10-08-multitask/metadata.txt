TITLE: Why Multitasking Mistakes Aren't Random: New Brain Study #shorts

DESCRIPTION:
Multitasking mistakes may not be lost focus. In a new Nature Neuroscience study, Cheng Xue and Marlene Cohen (University of Chicago) had more than 200 adults and two rhesus monkeys play a game where two stripe features changed at once, only one mattered, and the rule secretly switched. When the rule was uncertain, errors followed a pattern: signals for the irrelevant feature leaked into the relevant one, and people remembered the ignored detail better after a miss. The human side of the study is behavior only; brain activity was recorded in the monkeys. Xue suggests leaving a buffer between tasks. When did you last send the right thing to the wrong place?
