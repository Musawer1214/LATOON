# Sources and image credits (trees Short, 2026-10-31 #2)
## Facts (primary sources opened 2026-10-31)
- Wheeler & Stroock 2008, Nature 455:208, "The transpiration of water at negative pressures in a synthetic tree": https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07226 (abstract: cohesion-tension; xylem pressure often negative, liquid under tension, metastable; synthetic gel tree, flow at -1.0 MPa or lower)
- Scholander, Bradstreet, Hemmingsen & Hammel 1965, Science 148:339, "Sap Pressure in Vascular Plants": https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.148.3668.339 (abstract: "sap pressures during transpiration are normally negative, ranging from -4 or -5 atmospheres in a damp forest to -80 atmospheres in the desert")
- Dixon & Joly 1895, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 186:563, "On the ascent of sap": https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.1895.0012 (historical proposal; not quoted on screen)
- Koch, Sillett, Jennings & Davis 2004, Nature 428:851, "The limits to tree height": https://www.nature.com/articles/nature02417 (tallest known tree 112.7 m, a redwood)
- Hayward 1970, Nature 225:376, "Mechanical pump with a suction lift of 17 metres": https://www.nature.com/articles/225376b0 (at sea level the atmosphere supports a water column ~10.4 m; ordinary suction pumps limited near this; a special pump beat it, hence "ordinary")
- Choat et al. 2018, Nature 558:531, "Triggers of tree mortality under drought": https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0240-x (catastrophic hydraulic failure is a principal mechanism of tree mortality during drought)
