## Step 1 — what the idea IS (before any style)
THE IDEA: we can only ever see a machine from the outside (what it says, what it does), so the question "does it think?" is really
three questions: does it behave like us, does it build and use a picture of the world inside, and is anyone in there feeling anything.
Every chapter of the script is one more attempt to look past the outside: a game played through a wall (Turing), a machine that only
gives back what was put in (Lovelace), a move nobody put in (Move 37), a room whose inside is empty of meaning (Searle), a sealed box
we finally probe (Othello-GPT), a test passed from the outside (PNAS 2026), and the one room no probe can enter (consciousness).

So the picture the viewer must learn in the first minute and keep for 18 minutes is: **OUTSIDE vs INSIDE**.

## Step 3 — the world chosen for THIS topic
**"The Section Model."** An architect's daylight studio: every idea in the story is built as a scale model in plaster, card, oak and brass,
and most models are cut open in SECTION, the drawing architects use to show inside and outside at the same time. That is literally the
video's question, so the style IS the content: the camera always meets a thing from the outside first (what the judge sees), then glides
over the section cut to show the inside, and in the final chapter it reaches a room it cannot cut open.

- **Recurring device:** the WALL WITH A SLOT (an interface). Imitation game, Chinese Room, the sealed Othello box, the chat test and the
  final three doors are all the same idea: a wall, a slot, paper in and out.
- **Recurring symbols:** the carved wooden PARROT (rote/ratta: C1-06/07, C4-04, C5-08); the FOLDED MAP (an inner model: C5); the SAND
  TIMER (5 and 15 minutes); RED TOKENS (a vote "this one is human"); the LIT WINDOW (an inner life, C7-C8; never shown inside a machine).
- **Why not The Archive's look:** The Archive is a night library, candle key + moonlight, dark glass mascots. This film is **high-key
  daylight**, warm plaster and oak, long soft sun shadows from a big studio window, no characters with faces, no darkness until the C7 dusk
  (which is a deliberate story turn: the one thing we cannot see into). Same LATOON family: warm paper ground, SIGNAL RED accent,
  brass secondary, ink type, Neuron-star end frame.
- **Not clay again:** materials are plaster (matte, slightly chalky), card edges, oak, brass and paper, i.e. architectural maquette, not the
  soft clay-and-cord of sample 2. Figures are faceless architect's scale figures (no anatomy risk; real people appear only as credited photos).

### Staging and camera
- One continuous studio; chapters are different models on the same long table, joined by camera travel (no slideshow cuts). Within a
  chapter, cuts are motivated (section reveal, push into a slot, match cut paper -> paper).
- Camera grammar: OUTSIDE first (eye level, the judge's view) -> rise and glide over the section cut -> INSIDE close-up -> pull back to the
  whole model as the payoff wide. Log-space zooms, spring settles, never spins or whip-pans.
- Viewer prompts (C1-07 viva, C4-07 who understands?, C5-08 where would you draw the line?, C8-05 what would change your mind?) each get a
  "held" visual: the camera stops and the object waits for the viewer (an empty chair, a chalk ring, a blank chalk line, a shut door).
- On-screen text: language-neutral labels only (years, names, numbers, English terms), quote cards for exact quotes, small source tags.
