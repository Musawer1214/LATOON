- Popular information "Ice at the South Pole reveals cosmic particle accelerators" (65 billion solar neutrinos through your little fingernail per second; 1988 Halzen & Learned concept; why ice: dark, no life, low radioactivity, no earthquakes, existing station; hot-water "shower head" drilling; "lightbulb in reverse"; AMANDA bubbles, clear below 1,400 m, light travels ~300 m; 5,160 sensors on 86 cables, full size 2011; >100 million atmospheric particles/day; 2013 first evidence; NGC 1068 79 neutrinos, not yet definitive; Milky Way neutrinos; IceCube-Gen2 eight cubic km): https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2026/popular-information/

## Detector and construction
- IceCube detector page (5,160 DOMs, 86 strings, 60 per string, 1,450–2,450 m, 125 m hexagonal spacing, 17 m vertical; Cherenkov light; hot-water drill, 60 cm holes to 2,450 m, ~2 m/min, ~11 h deployment, refreeze ~couple of weeks; completed 18 Dec 2010; seven austral summers): https://icecube.wisc.edu/science/icecube/
- IceCube Quick Facts (~100 years for one interaction in a person-sized detector; ~48 h per hole; >750,000 L of water melted per hole; 2004–2010 construction; 275 atmospheric neutrinos/day): https://icecube.wisc.edu/about-us/facts/

## Physics note (standard textbook physics, not a single source)
- Cherenkov light: a charged particle moving faster than light's speed in the medium emits a cone of light (IceCube detector page above describes it for ice). Speed bars on screen are drawn without numbers (ice refractive index ≈ 1.3, so light in ice ≈ 3/4 of c).
