TITLE:
Catching a Ghost: The Nobel-Winning Telescope Made of Ice

DESCRIPTION:
Right now, about 65 billion neutrinos from the Sun are passing through your fingernail every second, and you feel nothing. So how do you catch a "ghost particle" that can fly through an entire planet? The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics went to Francis Halzen for one answer: IceCube, a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice at the South Pole, turned into a telescope.

In this video we follow the whole story: Pauli's "desperate remedy" in 1930, why neutrinos are so hard to catch, why deep polar ice is the perfect detector, how 86 holes were melted 2.5 km deep with hot water, how a faint blue Cherenkov flash reveals a neutrino's direction, and what IceCube found: the blazar TXS 0506+056, the hidden galaxy NGC 1068, and the first picture of the Milky Way in neutrinos. Plus what comes next with the IceCube Upgrade and the proposed IceCube-Gen2.
